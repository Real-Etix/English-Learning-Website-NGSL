## Definition
(logic) a statement that contradicts itself

## Examples
- `I always lie' is a paradox because if it is true it must be false _(wordnet)_

## Connections
- domain: academic
- builds_on: [[contradiction]] — Use 'paradox' when referring to a self-contradictory statement or situation that may be true, unlike 'contradiction' which often implies direct logical conflict.
- advanced_form: [[antinomy]] — Use 'antinomy' in formal or philosophical contexts to describe a paradox that arises from two equally valid but contradictory principles, rather than the more general 'paradox'.
- advanced_form: [[oxymoron]] — Use 'oxymoron' when the contradiction is expressed in a two-word phrase, especially in literary or rhetorical analysis; 'paradox' is broader and can refer to a statement or situation.
- advanced_form: [[conundrum]] — Use 'conundrum' to emphasize the puzzling or riddle-like nature of a paradox, often in everyday or informal contexts, when the contradiction is also a hard problem to solve.
